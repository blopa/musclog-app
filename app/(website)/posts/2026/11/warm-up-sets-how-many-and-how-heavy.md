For a working squat of 100 kg, a reasonable warm-up is three sets that climb toward the working weight: about 40%, 60% and 80% of it, with 5, 3 and 2 reps. On a barbell, Musclog also opens with an empty-bar set of 10 reps when the first ramp set is well above the bar. That makes four warm-up sets before your first working set. The sections below show the exact ramp the app uses, how it rounds the weights, and the cases where it adds nothing.

## The ramp for 100 kg

The app's ramp is a fixed list: 40% of the working weight for 5 reps, 60% for 3 reps, and 80% for 2 reps. Each set is heavier and shorter than the one before. Here it is for a 100 kg working set, with a 20 kg bar and the app's default plates, which are 25, 20, 15, 10, 5, 2.5 and 1.25 kg:

| Set         | Weight | Reps        | Plates per side |
| ----------- | ------ | ----------- | --------------- |
| Empty bar   | 20 kg  | 10          | none            |
| Warm-up 1   | 40 kg  | 5           | 10 kg           |
| Warm-up 2   | 60 kg  | 3           | 20 kg           |
| Warm-up 3   | 80 kg  | 2           | 30 kg           |
| Working set | 100 kg | your target | 40 kg           |

Each warm-up weight is a rounded version of the exact percentage, and here all of them round to exactly the figures above. Rest about 45 seconds between warm-up sets. The app treats that as its own short rest, shorter than between working sets, because nothing in a warm-up needs recovering from.

## Why the empty bar comes first

A barbell opens with the empty bar only when the lightest ramp set is at least 1.5 times the bar's weight. For a 20 kg bar, that is 30 kg. In the example, 40 kg qualifies, so the 10 reps on the bar come first. For a working weight that is light for the bar, the empty-bar set is not added, because the lightest ramp set would be too close to it to be worth a separate set.

These numbers are Musclog's choices. The app's stated reason for the empty bar is that it grooves the movement pattern before any load goes on.

## Rounding to real plates

Warm-up weights are rounded to something the gym can actually load. On a barbell the app uses your gym's bar and plates, and it never goes below the empty bar. Dumbbells and machines skip the bar and round to the gym's smallest increment, which is 2.5 kg by default. Without a gym set up, the app uses the default plates and step, so it still gives weights you can load.

Rounding can make two sets the same. When that happens, the duplicate is dropped. A light lift therefore gets fewer than three warm-up sets, which is intended.

## When to skip the ramp

The app adds no warm-up in three situations:

- **Bodyweight or unloaded exercises**, where there is no load to ramp.
- **A barbell working weight within about 15% of the bar.** Here the bar itself is the warm-up, and the app says so.
- **A weight too light for a ramp.** When every ramp step would round to the working weight or above, the app says the weight is too light for a ramp and you start at your working weight.

Also, a warm-up set within 10% of the working weight is dropped. A set at 90 kg for a 100 kg working set would be treated as the first working set, not a warm-up.

## Warm-ups do not count as work

Musclog counts warm-up sets separately from working sets. They do not add to volume, personal records, or the progression rules, which look only at the working sets you performed. They are kept in the session and in calories, because they are real effort, but they never become the "previous set" the next working set is based on. The reasoning is that warm-ups are light on purpose, so counting them would pull averages down and could make a light warm-up look like a record attempt.

## The rules, in short

- Three ramp sets at 40, 60 and 80% of the working weight, with 5, 3 and 2 reps.
- A barbell adds an empty-bar set of 10 reps when the lightest ramp set is at least 30 kg (1.5 times a 20 kg bar).
- Weights round to what your gym can load. Duplicates and sets within 10% of the working weight are dropped.
- Skip the ramp for bodyweight work, for a barbell weight near the bar, or for a very light set.
