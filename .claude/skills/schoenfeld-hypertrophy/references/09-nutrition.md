# Ch 9: Nutrition for Hypertrophy
Book: Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy (2nd ed) | Author: Brad Schoenfeld

## Core Principle
A positive energy balance combined with adequate protein is essential for maximizing hypertrophy. Carbs fuel performance; fats support hormones; timing matters less than totals.

---

## ENERGY BALANCE

### Deficit (Hypocaloric)
- Blunts mTOR signaling, activates AMPK/FOXO
- Muscle protein synthesis reduced ~19-27%
- CAN build muscle in deficit (especially untrained/overfat)
- Hypertrophy will be suboptimal vs surplus

### Maintenance (Eucaloric)
- Suboptimal for maximizing growth
- Amino acids diverted to vital organs in fasted state
- RT helps but doesn't fully overcome limitation

### Surplus (Hypercaloric)
- Potent anabolism stimulator even without exercise
- Combined with RT = optimal hypertrophy environment
- Fat gain is inevitable trade-off

### Surplus Recommendations
| Training Status | Daily Surplus |
|-----------------|---------------|
| Untrained | Up to 2,000 kcal (gains mostly lean) |
| Intermediate | 500-1,000 kcal |
| Advanced | 200-500 kcal (minimize fat gain) |

*Untrained can use larger surplus effectively; trained individuals deposit more as fat*

## PROTEIN

### Requirements
| Population | Daily Intake |
|------------|--------------|
| Minimum for hypertrophy | 1.6 g/kg/day |
| Upper beneficial range | 2.2 g/kg/day |
| During deficit | Higher end (2.2-2.4 g/kg/day) |

### Quality Considerations
- **Complete proteins**: All EAAs in sufficient quantity
- **Leucine**: Key trigger for MPS; ~2-3g per meal optimal
- **DIAAS**: Digestible indispensable amino acid score
- Animal sources generally superior to plant (higher DIAAS)

### Plant-Based Diets
- Require ~20% more total protein
- Combine complementary sources (legumes + grains)
- Consider leucine-fortified plant proteins
- Soy, pea, rice (combined) = viable options

### Per-Meal Protein
| Body Size | Protein Per Meal |
|-----------|------------------|
| Minimum | 0.4 g/kg |
| Optimal | 0.4-0.55 g/kg |
| Post-workout (elderly) | May benefit from higher (~0.6 g/kg) |

**Example**: 80 kg person → 32-44g protein per meal

---

## CARBOHYDRATES

### Functions
- Primary fuel for high-intensity exercise
- Glycogen storage (muscle and liver)
- Protein-sparing effect
- Insulin secretion (anticatabolic)

### Requirements
| Goal | Daily Intake |
|------|--------------|
| Minimum | 3 g/kg/day |
| Moderate training | 4-5 g/kg/day |
| High volume training | 5-7 g/kg/day |

### Timing
- Pre-workout: Ensures glycogen availability
- Intra-workout: Generally unnecessary for sessions <2 hours
- Post-workout: Replenishes glycogen; less urgent than believed

### Type
- Glycemic index less important than total intake
- Whole foods preferred for micronutrients/fiber
- Simple sugars fine around training window

---

## DIETARY FAT

### Functions
- Hormone production (testosterone precursor)
- Cell membrane integrity
- Fat-soluble vitamin absorption
- Essential fatty acids

### Requirements
| Measure | Recommendation |
|---------|----------------|
| Minimum | 0.5-1.0 g/kg/day |
| % of calories | 20-35% |

*Going below 15-20% of calories may impair testosterone*

### Types
- **Prioritize**: Monounsaturated (olive oil, avocado, nuts)
- **Include**: Polyunsaturated (fatty fish, flax, walnuts)
- **Limit**: Saturated (<10% of calories)
- **Avoid**: Trans fats

### Omega-3 Fatty Acids
- May have mild anabolic properties
- Anti-inflammatory effects support recovery
- 2-3g EPA+DHA daily suggested

## MEAL FREQUENCY & TIMING

### Meal Frequency
| Meals/Day | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| 3 | Adequate if protein is sufficient |
| 4-6 | May be optimal for maximizing MPS |
| >6 | No additional benefit |

- Spread protein intake across 4+ meals
- 3-4 hours between protein feedings optimal
- Each meal should contain ~0.4-0.55 g/kg protein

### Nutrient Timing (Anabolic Window)

**Pre-Workout (1-2 hours before)**
- Protein: 0.4-0.5 g/kg (20-40g)
- Carbs: Moderate serving for energy
- Fat: Keep low (slows digestion)

**Post-Workout**
- Less urgent than previously believed
- If training fasted: Consume protein ASAP (<45 min)
- If fed pre-workout: Within 4-6 hours of pre-workout meal
- Protein: 0.4-0.5 g/kg
- Carbs: 1.0-1.5 g/kg (if 2x/day training or glycogen-depleting)

**Practical Approach**
- Pre and post meals within 4-6 hours of each other
- Don't train fully fasted if possible
- If fasted training: Post-workout nutrition becomes more urgent

### Pre-Sleep Protein
- 30-40g casein or mixed protein before bed
- Supports overnight MPS
- May be especially beneficial for elderly

---

## HYDRATION

### Requirements
- 30-45 ml/kg/day baseline
- Additional for sweat losses during training
- Thirst is generally reliable indicator

### Effects of Dehydration
- >2% body mass loss = impaired performance
- Cell volume affects anabolic signaling
- Stay consistently hydrated vs dramatic swings

---

## SUPPLEMENTS

### Evidence-Based Options
| Supplement | Benefit | Dose |
|------------|---------|------|
| Creatine monohydrate | Increased training capacity, cell hydration | 3-5g/day |
| Protein powders | Convenient protein source | As needed for targets |
| Caffeine | Performance enhancement | 3-6 mg/kg pre-workout |
| Beta-alanine | Buffering capacity | 3-6g/day |
| Citrulline | Blood flow, performance | 6-8g/day |

### Overrated/Unproven
- BCAAs (unnecessary if protein adequate)
- Testosterone boosters (ineffective)
- Most "mass gainers" (expensive carbs)
- HMB (minimal benefit in trained lifters)

---

## TAKE-HOME POINTS

1. **Energy surplus** necessary for maximal hypertrophy (200-500 kcal for trained)
2. **Protein**: 1.6-2.2 g/kg/day; spread across 4+ meals of 0.4-0.55 g/kg each
3. **Carbs**: Minimum 3 g/kg/day; more for higher training volumes
4. **Fat**: 20-35% of calories; prioritize unsaturated sources
5. **Timing**: Consume protein both pre- and post-workout within 4-6 hour window
6. **Fasted training**: Consume protein ASAP post-workout (<45 min)
7. **Vegans**: Need ~20% more protein; combine complementary sources
