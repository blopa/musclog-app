# Ch 1: Hypertrophy-Related Responses and Adaptations to Exercise Stress
Book: Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy (2nd ed) | Author: Brad Schoenfeld

## Core Principle
Muscle growth requires understanding neuromuscular system responses and adaptations. Early gains = neural; later gains = muscular.

---

## NEUROMUSCULAR SYSTEM

### Muscle Structure
- Fibers organized: epimysium → perimysium → endomysium
- Fibers multinucleated; can span 600mm length
- Sarcomeres = basic contractile unit; actin + myosin = ~50% muscle protein
- Myofibrils contain hundreds to thousands of sarcomeres in series

### Motor Units
- Motor unit = motor neuron + all fibers it innervates
- All-or-none principle: when activated, all fibers in unit contract
- Size principle (Henneman): smaller/slower units recruited first → larger/faster as force demands increase

### Fiber Types
| Type | Contraction | Force | Fatigue Resistance | Growth Potential |
|------|-------------|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| Type I | Slow (~110ms peak) | Low | High | Moderate |
| Type IIa | Moderate (~50ms) | Moderate | Moderate | High |
| Type IIx | Fast | High | Low | Highest |

- Average human: ~50/50 split Type I/II
- Type II fibers show ~50% greater hypertrophic response than Type I
- Muscle-specific: soleus ~80% Type I; triceps ~60% Type II
- Women often have larger Type I than Type IIa fibers

## NEURAL ADAPTATIONS

### Early-Phase Adaptations (first weeks)
Primary drivers of initial strength gains:

1. **Increased neural drive**: Greater EMG amplitude; improved voluntary activation
2. **Enhanced recruitment**: Better ability to activate high-threshold motor units
3. **Rate coding**: Increased motor unit firing frequency (15-49% increases reported)
4. **Synchronization**: Simultaneous discharge of multiple motor units (contribution modest)
5. **Reduced antagonist coactivation**: Less opposition from opposing muscles
6. **Doublets**: Paired spikes <5ms apart; increase from ~5% to ~33% of motor units

### Recruitment Thresholds
- Upper limit ~85% of maximal isometric force
- Heavy loads (>70% 1RM): require high-threshold motor units
- Light loads lifted explosively: can recruit high-threshold units even at 33% max
- Fatigue: progressively decreases recruitment threshold of high-threshold units

## PROTEIN BALANCE

### Transcription & Translation
- Protein synthesis = transcription (nucleus) + translation (ribosomes)
- Translation initiation = rate-limiting step
- Ribosomes: ~20% cell volume; ~85% total cellular RNA

### Exercise Response
- During training: protein synthesis suppressed, proteolysis elevated (net negative)
- Post-exercise: MPS elevated 2-5x for 48+ hours
- Net positive balance when sufficient recovery between sessions

### Ribosome Biogenesis
- Critical for long-term growth
- Ribosomal pool limited; must expand to support continued hypertrophy
- Strong correlations between ribosome content and muscle growth

## HYPERTROPHY TYPES

### Parallel Hypertrophy (primary)
- Sarcomeres added in parallel → increased fiber diameter
- Main mechanism for resistance training–induced growth
- Increases muscle cross-sectional area

### Serial Hypertrophy
- Sarcomeres added in series → increased fascicle length
- Observed in early training (~10% fascicle length increase in 35 days)
- Adaptations may not persist beyond first 5 weeks
- High-speed training may promote serial addition

### Sarcoplasmic Hypertrophy
- Increase in noncontractile elements: glycogen, water, organelles
- Bodybuilders show higher glycogen (2x untrained) and connective tissue than powerlifters
- Training can increase intracellular water (glycogen attracts 3-4g water per gram)
- High-volume training may elicit disproportionate sarcoplasmic vs myofibrillar growth

## SATELLITE CELLS

### Function
- Myogenic stem cells between basal lamina and sarcolemma
- Normally quiescent; activated by mechanical stimulus
- Produce daughter cells → self-renew OR differentiate → myoblasts → fuse to existing fibers
- Response peaks 72-96 hours post-workout

### Importance for Hypertrophy
- Donate nuclei to myofibers (maintain DNA-to-protein ratio)
- May be critical when hypertrophy exceeds ~15-26%
- Extreme responders show much greater satellite cell expansion
- Strong positive relationship between satellite cell response and muscle growth

### Muscle Memory
- Myonuclei appear retained even after detraining/atrophy
- Previously trained muscle regains size faster (cellular memory mechanism)

---

## HYPERPLASIA

- Fiber number increase; evidence mainly from animal models
- Extreme loading (stretch overload) in birds: up to 82% fiber increase
- Human evidence: minimal to none with traditional training
- If occurs, contribution to total hypertrophy appears negligible

## HORMONES

### IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1)
- Three isoforms: IGF-1Ea, IGF-1Eb (systemic), IGF-1Ec/MGF (local)
- Activates PI3K/Akt pathway
- Primary role: stimulate satellite cell differentiation/fusion
- Not essential for exercise-induced hypertrophy (IGF-1 deficient mice still respond)

### Growth Hormone
- Pulsatile release; highest during sleep
- Anabolic effects mainly via IGF-1 potentiation
- Primary direct effect on collagen synthesis, not myofibrillar protein
- Exercise can elevate 10-290x above baseline (metabolite-mediated)

### Testosterone
- Men ~10x women's levels
- Normal range: 264-916 ng/dL
- Direct effects: ↑ protein synthesis, ↓ proteolysis
- Indirect effects: potentiates GH, IGF-1 release
- Increases satellite cell number
- Hypogonadism impairs muscle building; within-range variations less clear

### Insulin
- Primary hypertrophic role: ↓ protein breakdown (not ↑ synthesis)
- Manipulated mainly through nutrition, not exercise

### Acute vs Chronic Hormonal Response
- "Hormone hypothesis": post-exercise spikes drive growth
- Evidence: mostly equivocal to unsupportive
- If effect exists, appears modest/permissive at best
- Local factors (mechanotransduction, myokines) likely more important

---

## MYOKINES

### Mechano Growth Factor (MGF)
- Splice variant of IGF-1 specific to muscle
- Expressed in response to mechanical loading
- Kick-starts post-exercise recovery
- Extreme responders: 126% increase; nonresponders: unchanged
- Stimulates MPS via PI3K/Akt; suppresses catabolic FOXO
- Early-stage satellite cell activation and proliferation

### Interleukin-6 (IL-6)
- Upregulated up to 100x by resistance training
- Correlates with hypertrophic adaptations
- Primary actions: satellite cell proliferation, myonuclear accretion
- Chronic elevation = catabolic; acute elevation = anabolic

### Myostatin
- Negative regulator of muscle mass
- Mutations → extreme muscularity (Belgian Blue cattle)
- Resistance training downregulates myostatin (3x greater reduction in trained)
- Acts via SMAD2/3; inhibits Akt/mTOR pathway

### Other Myokines
- **HGF**: Only known activator of quiescent satellite cells
- **LIF**: Satellite cell proliferation; prevents premature differentiation
- **IL-15**: May regulate oxidative/fatigue properties (role unclear)

---

## TAKE-HOME POINTS

1. Early strength gains = neural (recruitment, rate coding, synchronization)
2. Muscle growth = net protein balance over time
3. Primary hypertrophy = parallel (sarcomeres added in parallel)
4. Satellite cells crucial for sustained/maximal hypertrophy
5. Hyperplasia contribution minimal in humans
6. Acute hormonal spikes: minor/permissive role at best
7. Myokines (MGF, IL-6) important local regulators of growth
