# Ch 8: Program Design for Maximal Hypertrophy
Book: Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy (2nd ed) | Author: Brad Schoenfeld

## Core Principle
Effective hypertrophy programming combines biomechanical understanding with strategic periodization. The art is adapting scientific principles to individual needs.

---

## BIOMECHANICAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Length-Tension Relationship
- Optimal force: ~125-140% resting length (moderate stretch)
- **Active insufficiency**: Two-joint muscle shortened at both ends = weak
- **Passive tension**: Two-joint muscle stretched at one end = stronger

**Application**:
- Incline curls (shoulder extended) = greater biceps long head tension
- Overhead triceps (shoulder flexed) = greater long head stretch
- Stiff-leg deadlift = greater hamstring tension than leg curl

### Training Angle
- Changes muscle activation patterns and regional stress
- Incline vs flat vs decline = different pec regions
- Different angles target different heads of deltoids

### Plane of Movement
- Sagittal (front-back): Presses, rows, curls
- Frontal (side-to-side): Lateral raises, side lunges
- Transverse (rotation): Flyes, cable crossovers

### Hand/Foot Spacing
- Wide grip = greater stretch, different muscle emphasis
- Narrow grip = different leverage, often more triceps
- Stance width affects quad/glute/adductor emphasis

### Exercise Type
- **Free weights**: Greater stabilizer activation; functional carryover
- **Machines**: Better isolation; safer to failure
- **Cables**: Constant tension throughout ROM
- **Use all types** for complete development

| Region | Emphasized By |
|--------|---------------|
| Upper (clavicular) | Incline presses, low-to-high flyes |

| Muscle | Exercises |
|--------|-----------|

| Head | Exercises |
|------|-----------|

### Arms
- **Biceps long head**: Incline curls (shoulder extended)
- **Biceps short head**: Preacher curls (shoulder flexed)
- **Triceps long head**: Overhead extensions
- **Triceps lateral/medial**: Pushdowns, close-grip press

### Legs
| Muscle | Exercises |
|--------|-----------|

| Hamstrings | RDLs, leg curls (seated vs lying) |
| Glutes | Hip thrusts, lunges, deep squats |

## PERIODIZATION

### Purpose
- Manage fatigue accumulation
- Provide progressive overload
- Prevent accommodation/staleness
- Optimize recovery and supercompensation

### Models

**Linear Periodization**
- Volume decreases, intensity increases over mesocycle
- Example: Wk 1-4 (3x12), Wk 5-8 (4x8), Wk 9-12 (5x5)
- Good for beginners; predictable progression

**Undulating Periodization**
- Volume/intensity varies within week
- Daily undulating (DUP): Different rep ranges each session
- Example: Mon (3x10), Wed (4x6), Fri (2x15)
- May provide greater stimulus variety

**Reverse Linear**
- Intensity decreases, volume increases over time
- May be beneficial for hypertrophy focus
- Less commonly used

**Block Periodization**
- Concentrated focus on specific quality per block
- Accumulation (high volume) → Transmutation → Realization
- Good for advanced lifters

### Deloads
- Planned reduction in volume/intensity
- **Timing**: Every 3-6 weeks depending on individual
- **Duration**: Typically 1 week
- **Reduction**: 40-60% of normal volume; maintain intensity
- Purpose: Recovery, resensitization, injury prevention

## PROGRAM STRUCTURE

### Split Options

**Total Body (3x/week)**
- Good for beginners
- Each muscle 3x/week
- Lower volume per session
- Example: Mon/Wed/Fri - all muscles

**Upper/Lower (4x/week)**
- Each muscle 2x/week
- Moderate volume per session
- Example: Mon/Thu upper; Tue/Fri lower

**Push/Pull/Legs (6x/week)**
- Each muscle 2x/week
- Higher volume per session possible
- Example: Push/Pull/Legs repeated

**Body Part Split (5-6x/week)**
- Traditional bodybuilding approach
- High volume per muscle, 1x/week frequency
- Example: Chest/Back/Shoulders/Arms/Legs

### Weekly Volume Distribution
| Weekly Sets/Muscle | Frequency Needed |
|-------------------|------------------|
| 10-12 | 1-2x/week sufficient |
| 15-20 | 2x/week optimal |
| 25-30+ | 3x/week necessary |

## SAMPLE TEMPLATES

### Beginner (3x/week Total Body)
- 2-3 exercises per major muscle group
- 2-3 sets per exercise
- 8-12 reps
- ~10 sets/muscle/week
- Progress by adding weight when hitting rep targets

### Intermediate (4x/week Upper/Lower)
**Upper A**: Horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push, arms
**Upper B**: Vertical pull, incline push, rows, arms
**Lower A**: Squat pattern, hip hinge, leg curl, calves
**Lower B**: Hip hinge, single leg, leg extension, calves
- 3-4 sets per exercise
- 6-12 reps
- 15-20 sets/muscle/week

### Advanced (6x/week PPL)
Higher volume, exercise variety, intensity techniques
- 4-5 exercises per session
- 3-4 sets per exercise
- Various rep ranges (6-20)
- 20-25+ sets/muscle/week
- Periodized intensity techniques

## EXERCISE ORDER

### Principles
1. **Prioritize weak points**: Train lagging muscles first
2. **Compounds before isolation**: When fresh for heavy loads
3. **Free weights before machines**: Higher coordination demand
4. **Large muscles before small**: Unless prioritizing small muscles

### Exception
- If a small muscle is lagging, train it first when fresh

---

## TAKE-HOME POINTS

1. **Biomechanics matter**: Length-tension, angles, planes affect muscle development
2. **Exercise variety**: Multiple movements per muscle for complete development
3. **Periodization**: Essential for continued progress; multiple valid models
4. **Deloads**: Integrate every 3-6 weeks; ~50% volume reduction
5. **Individual adaptation**: Apply principles based on personal needs/response
6. **Keep complex exercises consistent**: Rotate isolation more freely
