# Ch 2: Mechanisms of Hypertrophy
Book: Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy (2nd ed) | Author: Brad Schoenfeld

## Core Principle
Three primary mechanisms drive hypertrophy: mechanical tension (most important), metabolic stress, and muscle damage. Their relative contributions and interactions remain debated.

---

## MECHANICAL TENSION

### The Primary Driver
- Most important factor in training-induced hypertrophy
- Directly stimulates mTOR via ERK/TSC2 pathway
- Mediated by phosphatidic acid synthesis

### Mechanosensors Respond To:
- **Magnitude** of loading (peak tension predicts MAPK phosphorylation)
- **Duration** (time under tension: linear relationship with JNK signaling)
- **Type** of load (stretch vs dynamic; eccentric vs concentric vs isometric)

### Mechanotransduction
Mechanical forces → chemical signals via:
- **Integrins**: Primary mechanosensors at cell surface
- **Focal adhesion kinase (FAK)**: Key signal initiator; load-dependent
- **Titin**: Stiffness-dependent signaling
- G protein-coupled receptors
- Nuclear deformation (YAP translocation)

---

## SIGNALING PATHWAYS

### Anabolic Pathways

**PI3K/Akt Pathway**
- Master regulator of muscle growth
- Activates mTOR → p70S6K → protein synthesis
- Inhibits FOXO → reduces proteolysis
- Suppresses GSK3β → activates eIF2B
- Akt1 most responsive to mechanical stimuli
- Not obligatory; mTOR can be activated independently

**mTOR**
- Critical for mechanically-induced hypertrophy
- Two complexes: mTORC1 (rapamycin-sensitive) and mTORC2
- Targets: p70S6K, 4E-BP1 → translation initiation
- Energy/nutrient sensor; elevated energy promotes activation
- Persistently elevated basal mTOR may impair growth

**MAPK Pathways**
- ERK1/2: Satellite cell proliferation; synergistic with PI3K
- p38 MAPK: Primarily aerobic; p38γ specific to slow-twitch fibers
- JNK: Most sensitive to mechanical tension; responsive to eccentrics

**Calcium-Dependent**
- Calcineurin: Promotes hypertrophy in all fiber types; slow-twitch phenotype shift
- CaMKII/IV: Mediates growth and mitochondrial biogenesis

**Phosphatidic Acid**
- Lipid second messenger
- Directly binds mTOR; activates p70S6K
- Highly sensitive to mechanical stimulation (stretch, eccentrics)

### Catabolic Pathways

**AMPK**
- Cellular energy sensor; activated by high AMP/ATP ratio
- Suppresses protein synthesis (antagonizes mTOR)
- Stimulates proteolysis (activates FOXO, atrogin-1)
- Strong inverse correlation with hypertrophy magnitude

**MSTN-SMAD**
- Myostatin = potent negative regulator
- Signals via SMAD2/3 → inhibits transcription
- Resistance training decreases MSTN expression

## METABOLIC STRESS

### Definition
Accumulation of metabolites: lactate, inorganic phosphate, H+

### When It Occurs
- Anaerobic glycolysis dominant (15-120 sec efforts)
- Multiple sets of 8-12 reps with short rest
- Blood flow restriction (BFR) training
- Training under hypoxia

### Evidence
- BFR training: marked hypertrophy with loads <40% 1RM
- Bodybuilders show muscle mass equal to/greater than powerlifters despite lighter loads
- Correlation between metabolite accumulation and CSA increases

### Proposed Mechanisms

1. **Increased Fiber Recruitment**
   - Fatigue decreases recruitment thresholds
   - Higher-threshold motor units progressively recruited
   - However, fiber recruitment alone may not fully explain effects

2. **Cell Swelling**
   - Lactate accumulation → osmotic changes → cellular hydration
   - Pressure against membrane perceived as threat
   - Triggers anabolic signaling to reinforce structure
   - Fast-twitch fibers especially sensitive (high AQP4 content)

3. **Altered Myokine Production**
   - May upregulate anabolic / downregulate catabolic myokines
   - Evidence mixed for IL-6, MSTN
   - BFR shown to downregulate FOXO3A, atrogin-1, MuRF-1

4. **Systemic Hormone Production**
   - Strong association with GH elevation (up to 290-fold)
   - Mediated by lactate/H+ accumulation
   - If effect exists, appears minor/permissive

### Key Caveat
Whether metabolic stress is additive or redundant to mechanical tension remains unclear.

## MUSCLE DAMAGE (EIMD)

### Characteristics
- Ranges from molecular disruption to large tears
- Followed by inflammation, Ca2+ disturbance, protein breakdown
- Creatine kinase elevation peaks 48 hours to 4-6 days post-workout

### Eccentric Emphasis
- Eccentrics cause most damage
- More prevalent in fast-twitch fibers
- Due to mechanical disruption of actomyosin bonds

### Repeated Bout Effect
- Damage attenuates rapidly with same exercise
- Just one additional bout reduces swelling to ~1/3
- By 5 sessions, damage practically inconsequential
- Effects last months even without training

### Proposed Mechanisms

1. **Inflammatory Processes**
   - Neutrophils: Remove debris; may signal other inflammatory cells
   - Macrophages: Secrete local growth factors (VEGF, IGF-1)
   - ROS: May activate MAPK, enhance IGF-1 signaling

2. **Satellite Cell Activity**
   - Damaged fibers require additional nuclei
   - Signaling via nitric oxide + HGF
   - COX-2 pathway promotes satellite cell proliferation/fusion
   - NSAIDs may blunt satellite cell activity

3. **IGF-1 Production**
   - Eccentric exercise elevates MGF mRNA at 24h, IGF-1Ea/Eb at 72h
   - Positively correlated with damage markers

4. **Cell Swelling**
   - Inflammatory response causes fluid buildup
   - Arm circumference can increase up to 9%
   - Persists 48+ hours even in trained individuals

### Optimal Level
- Mild-moderate damage may enhance hypertrophy
- Excessive damage: reduces force capacity, impairs training, delays recovery
- Threshold exists beyond which no additional benefit

### NSAIDs and Hypertrophy
- Occasional use: unlikely to impair gains
- Chronic high-dose use (1,200mg/day ibuprofen): may blunt hypertrophy ~50%
- IL-6 downregulation as possible mechanism
- Elderly with chronic inflammation may benefit; healthy trained may not

---

## TAKE-HOME POINTS

1. **Mechanical tension** = clearly most important factor
2. **Metabolic stress** shows compelling evidence for contribution; mechanisms include fiber recruitment, cell swelling, myokines
3. **Muscle damage** may enhance adaptations but excessive damage is detrimental
4. Whether these mechanisms are additive or redundant remains undetermined
5. mTOR is critical to mechanically-induced hypertrophy
6. AMPK acts as metabolic switch opposing anabolism
